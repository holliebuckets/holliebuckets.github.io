Audio-Spatial Task Guidance

The goal

I wanted to know whether voice alone can teach a hands-on task, and what has to change before people trust it, before anyone builds the real system.

This study simulates hands-free audio and wearable camera guidance that walks someone through tying a tie. A researcher plays the system from behind the curtain (the Wizard of Oz method), which let us test with novice and expert groups without building anything first. Three findings came out of it, listed below, and all three point to trust calibration as the hard part. Build 3.1, a branching prototype with audio, picks the work up from there.

Approach
  • Finding I: people couldn't tell what state the system was in, so they leaned on the proctor.
  • Finding II: experts got in their own way. Knowing the task well made the guidance harder to follow.
  • Finding III: audio alone is ambiguous about how things sit in space.
  • Each finding is tied back to the literature on grounding, earcons, expertise, and trust in automation.
Build
  • Build 3.1, a branching HTML prototype with audio, lives in its own repository.
  • The findings deck is a standalone interactive presentation authored for research evaluation.
  • The synthesis names trust calibration as the problem to design against.

Multi-Agent Incubation Pipeline

The goal

Take an idea to a vetted, built, and documented prototype, and ask for a person only where judgment matters, at four named gates.

Two AI review boards look at each concept in turn. One focuses on research integrity and the other on build quality, and they argue directly when they disagree. What comes out is a working prototype, a presentation, a concept field guide, and a decision log that records why each choice was made. It's in beta after one full run on a real example.

Approach
  • I treat the process as something to design: stages, gates, and a decision log that shows why choices were made.
  • It produces a prototype, an HTML presentation, and a concept field guide.
  • It ships with two ready-made rosters you can fork for another domain.
Build
  • The Vetting Board and Build Board seats are dispatched as autonomous sub-agents with dedicated prompts.
  • If your environment can't dispatch reliably, there's a disclosed, spot-checked alternative mode.
  • It has run end to end once, on a single worked example. The release notes list what's proven and what's still open.

On-Wrist Ambient Companion

The goal

Explore what a calm, unobtrusive personal AI could be on the wrist. It collects the day through sensors and a single gesture, and speaks up only when there's a story worth telling.

This project isn't a journaling app or a tracker. I designed it as an ambient personal correspondent on the wrist. The watch gathers fragments, an AI engine decides whether there's a story in them, and the phone presents it to read and annotate. An editorial Constitution governs when the system speaks and what it won't say.

Approach
  • Ten dispatch types, each documented as a full UX walk-through from moment to reading.
  • An editorial Constitution of ten Articles governs how the app decides what to say.
  • A mycelium palette set in New York, Optima, and SF Mono, drawn from a world where plant intelligence won.
  • Anything that needs clinical or legal sign-off is designed but held back, and the docs say so.
Build
  • The watch captures HealthKit heart rate, CoreLocation points of interest, and CoreMotion context. Contextual gesture and on-screen triggers initiate capture sessions.
  • WCSession connects watch and phone, with an offline queue as a fallback.
  • Secure token authentication, with generative dispatches routed through a local foundation model proxy.
  • Automated workspace generation, written architectural standards, and handoff notes in the repository.

On-Device Homography & Perspective Rectification

The goal

Let a prototype know exactly where a known image sits in a camera view, using only public Vision and CoreImage APIs, so spatial experiences don't depend on ARKit.

Give the tool a reference (a phone screenshot, a poster, a painting) and a scene photo, and it returns the pixel region and the perspective transform that maps one onto the other. You can copy the result straight into code, and the project comes with a short course so other people can learn the technique.

Approach
  • Three views for inspecting a match: the scene with corner pins, the reference spec, and a dual view that shows the un-warped patch beside it.
  • A coordinate HUD copies the result as a Swift snippet in one tap.
  • A 9-slide masterclass deck, a syllabus, and speaker notes come with it for teaching.
Build
  • Four passes: text recognition, rectangle detection, a hue-histogram plus FeaturePrint ranking, then a DLT homography solve.
  • The result is a 3x3 simd_float3x3 that maps reference coordinates into the scene.
  • Aspect-fit calibration converts between Vision space and UIKit space, and CIPerspectiveCorrection does the rectification.

Hands-Free Wearable Guidance Specification

The goal

Define what a hands-free wearable guide should do for physical tasks in the kitchen, the gym, and the workshop, and give teams a way to decide which use cases to build first.

I wrote the spec like a game design document. It follows one person through a full day and three scenarios in detail, then pulls out what they share: architecture, failure modes, privacy, and performance targets. A companion calculator sizes each use case by effort.

Approach
  • Three scenarios, moment by moment: poaching an egg, loading a barbell, and replacing a small sensor coin battery.
  • A user persona, a full-day timeline, emotional beats, and the moments where context switches.
  • Failure modes and recovery paths designed in from the start.
Build
  • System states, API calls, and sensor-activation patterns for each scenario.
  • Shared architecture, privacy and security considerations, and performance benchmarks.
  • An interactive calculator that sizes candidate use cases by effort.

Multimodal Model Evaluation Pipeline

The goal

Make it quick and repeatable to test how a model listens to audio and responds to different prompts, so differences between runs and versions are visible instead of half-remembered.

It's an automated command-line evaluation tool communicating with foundation model inference services, plus an eval system that builds reports. Recordings go in, timestamped reports come out and publish on their own, and an LLM judge and stored prompt evaluations let you compare runs. It's the model-behaviour side of my work: watching how responses change across prompts and versions.

Approach
  • One command turns a recording into a report page you can share.
  • Prompt evaluations are stored so runs can be compared.
Build
  • It takes audio or text prompts, has adjustable token limits, and runs one-shot or interactive.
  • The transcription scripts went through five iterations, and there's an LLM judge and backfill tooling.
  • Comprehensive evaluation suite with automated report publishing.

The goal

Explore whether a voice-first conversational assistant in wearable audio can sustain natural turn-taking, acoustic scene awareness, and spatial ergonomics.

Multimodal Generative Craft Companion

The goal

Explore how generative AI can turn an image, a few creative prompts, and an age group into a doable, timed craft project for anyone from preschool to adult.

This project is a native iOS application. You pick an input (camera, Files, paste, or drag and drop), an age group, and a few prompts, and a generative model returns a titled project with timed, step-by-step instructions. I built it for live demos, so it falls back gracefully when authentication lapses.

Approach
  • Three simple inputs: a source image, an age group from Preschool to Adult, and a shuffled set of creative prompts.
  • The output is a titled project with timed steps, each short enough to follow in one sitting.
  • You can bring your own content by drag and drop, paste, camera, or the Files app.
Build
  • A SwiftUI MVVM app generated with XcodeGen, with ad-hoc signing for quick installs on a device.
  • A developer menu lets me manage model gateway credentials directly on device.
  • If authentication expires mid-demo, the app falls back gracefully.

2023 · Feasibility

Multi-Modal Sensor Feasibility

I modeled interaction latency, computational constraints, and spatial tracking feasibility across conceptual sensor topologies, establishing rapid cross-functional evaluation rubrics for early-stage interactive prototypes.

Indoor wayfinding

Concentric precision

I defined indoor wayfinding requirements by scale: the building, then the room or exhibit, then the artifact, along with the sensors and data each level needs. I worked through airport and museum scenarios, and I separated wayfinding (orienting and traveling) from A-to-B navigation (following a path someone already knows).

2024–25 · Model evaluation

Evaluating generative image models

I studied how prompt structure, negative prompts, and image conditioning change what a model generates. A style prefix held up better than a suffix. Prompting alone couldn't control composition, but image conditioning could counter the model's habit of generating from the center. Negative prompts stopped working after about four terms. A character portfolio compared outputs across person, masculine, and feminine presentations.

Model evaluation

LLM rewrite vs rule-based trimming

I compared an LLM rewrite against a rule-based dependency parser for editing prompts, and sketched UI options for each. Across twelve test cases the rule-based approach got six right and the LLM got one. The LLM also hallucinated when a noun was missing.

2021 · Human factors research

Cognition, behaviour, and design

A research series on how people move through and make choices in mapped and unmapped spaces. It starts from behavioural ecology and covers spatial memory (landmarks, route, survey), egocentric and allocentric orientation, signal detection and cognitive load, and choice and priming.

Behaviour-aware assistance

Never Walk Alone

Interaction research exploring intent recognition in mobile assistants, analyzing real-time motion and context cues (CoreMotion) to distinguish confident movement from hesitation and dynamically adapt assistance.
